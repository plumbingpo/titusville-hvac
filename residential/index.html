Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of HVAC failures give quiet warnings long before the system quits. Watch for rising utility bills, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or a bedroom that never catches up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than an emergency replacement.

How old is your system? When a furnace or AC gets past the 12-year mark, it's worth thinking about the next one before the current one decides for you. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when options are few and everyone is uncomfortable. Our technicians know how the Titusville climate affects equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Titusville Homes

A Titusville HVAC maintenance visit is more than a filter change. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Minor faults are caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your system keeps running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge takes strain off the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Compared with the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Right-Sized Systems

Comfort Sized to Your Home's Demand

Bigger isn't better when it comes to HVAC. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, burns extra energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs constantly and still can't keep up on the hottest days. That's why we never just copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Picking a new system comes down to more than efficiency ratings. We'll compare heat pumps against a furnace-and-AC pair, and ducted against ductless, walking through operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Growing families frequently do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The HVAC Repairs Titusville Homes Need Most

Most repair calls we get in Titusville come down to a handful of recurring problems. The air conditioner blows warm air. The furnace won't light, or it lights and shuts off minutes later. The evaporator coil freezes solid, the condenser hums without starting, or a mini-split starts flashing error codes. Add refrigerant leaks, clogged drains, and forgetful thermostats, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On cooling systems, it's usually a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it's more often an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaking ductwork bleeding conditioned air into the attic gets sealed, and we recover and recharge the refrigerant once the leak itself is repaired. A few of these problems are simply a nuisance, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a genuine safety or water-damage risk, and we treat it accordingly.
